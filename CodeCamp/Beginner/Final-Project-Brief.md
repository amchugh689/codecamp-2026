# Beginner Final Project: Build Your Website

This is it, the final project! You've learned HTML, CSS and JavaScript this week. Now it's time to bring it all together and build a website that's entirely your own.

Remember the Freestyle page you built during the tutorials? Think of this as the full version. Instead of one page, you're building a small site with multiple pages that link to each other, styled the way you want, with at least one bit of JavaScript that actually does something.

## What to build

Pick a topic you're interested in and build a website around it. It's completely up to you what the site is about.

### Make sure your site includes:

- **At least 3 pages** that link to each other (e.g. a home page, an about page, and a page about your topic)
- **HTML structure** using headers, paragraphs, images, links, and divs to organise your content
- **CSS styling**, ideally in its own stylesheet, so your site has a consistent look across all its pages
- **At least one working piece of JavaScript** that responds to something the user does, like clicking a button, filling in a form, or updating part of the page

## What you'll be assessed on

- Code structure (HTML, CSS)
- UI Design (CSS)
- Functionality (JavaScript)
- Complexity

## Presenting your project

At the end of the week, you'll demo your website for about 3-4 minutes. Show off what you built and walk through a bit of your code. Your project doesn't need to be finished to present it. If you run out of time, it's fine to talk through what you were going for and how far you got.

Be ready to answer questions like:

- What was the biggest challenge you faced building this?
- If you had more time, what would you add or improve?

## A note on AI tools

You can use AI to help you learn, debug, or generate things like images or placeholder text. But the site needs to be your own work. If you use AI to generate your whole website without understanding or writing any of it yourself, it won't score well, and you'll be asked to explain your code during your demo, so make sure you understand what it does.

## Stuck for ideas?

Here are a few starting points if you're not sure what to build:

- **Personal hub**: a home page, an about page, and a page or two about your hobbies or interests
- **Mini app**: a simple to-do list, quiz, or calculator with some JavaScript to make it interactive
- **Fan site**: a multi-page site about a band, game, movie, or sports team, with different sections for different topics
- **Local guide**: a site about your town, a local event, or a place you love, split across a few pages
- **Recipe or hobby site**: a home page linking to a few pages, each covering a different recipe, project, or activity

Whatever you pick, make it your own. The more it reflects something you actually care about, the more fun it'll be to build.
