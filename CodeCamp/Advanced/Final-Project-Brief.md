# Advanced Final Project: Build Your Own Game


For your final project, you'll use those same building blocks you learned in the tutorials to build a game of your own. This needs to be an original idea, not a modified version of one of the tutorial games. Don't submit a reskinned Snake or a Pong clone with different colours. Pick a different concept entirely, even a simple one, and build it from scratch.

Reusing techniques you learned (a collision detection function, a scoring system, how you structured your game loop) is exactly what they're there for. What should be original is the game itself: the concept, how it plays, and its look and feel.

## What your game needs

Whatever idea you come up with, your game should have:

- A game loop that runs smoothly
- Controls that respond to the player
- A clear win, lose, or end condition
- A way to track score or progress
- A way to restart or play again
- Rules simple enough that someone else could pick it up and understand how to play without you explaining it first
## What you'll be assessed on
- Core gameplay and mechanics
- Completeness (win condition, scores, restart)
- Creativity
- Code quality
- Polish & visual presentation
- Demo & code explanation

## Stuck for an original idea?

Here are some directions that are different from anything covered in the tutorials:

- **Maze escape**: navigate a maze to reach an exit while avoiding obstacles or traps
- **Reaction game**: targets pop up briefly and the player has to click or hit them before they disappear
- **Memory match**: flip cards or tiles to find matching pairs before running out of tries
- **Quiz game**: answer questions against the clock to rack up points
- **Dodge and survive**: avoid a growing number of obstacles for as long as possible
- **Mini racing or obstacle course**: get from start to finish, avoiding hazards along the way

These are just starting points. If you've got your own idea, go with it, as long as it's genuinely yours and not a tweak on Snake, Pong, the shooter, or Flappy Bird.

## Showing off your game

At the end of the week, you'll demo your game and talk through how it works. Be ready to explain:

- How your game works and what makes it your own idea
- The choices you made while building it
- What you'd add or improve with more time

A small original game that fully works is worth more than a big, ambitious one that's still broken. If you're running low on time, get a simple, complete version working first, then add extra features if you have time left.
